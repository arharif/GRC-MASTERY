## Executive Summary & Cheat Sheet

### 4.1 Five key takeaways

1. **The board oversees; management executes.**  
   Board members provide direction, challenge management and exercise oversight. Executives deliver the strategy.

2. **Accountability must be clear.**  
   Material risks, decisions and corrective actions need designated owners.

3. **Risk appetite guides decisions.**  
   Management should understand when exposure exceeds approved limits and requires escalation.

4. **Independent assurance matters.**  
   Internal audit helps the board understand whether governance, risk management and controls are working.

5. **Governance must lead to action.**  
   Reports and meetings create limited value unless they lead to decisions, accountability and measurable improvements.

### 4.2 Essential terminology

| Term | Simple meaning |
|:--|:--|
| **Corporate governance** | How an organisation is directed and controlled |
| **Board oversight** | How the board monitors and challenges management |
| **Accountability** | Being answerable for decisions and outcomes |
| **Delegation of authority** | Formal permission to make specified decisions |
| **Risk appetite** | How much and what types of risk an organisation is willing to take |
| **Audit Committee** | Committee responsible for defined financial reporting, control and assurance oversight |
| **Risk Committee** | Committee responsible for defined risk oversight |
| **RACI** | Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed |
| **Board charter** | Document defining a board's roles and responsibilities |
| **Independent assurance** | Objective evaluation that increases confidence in governance and controls |

### Common failures and better practices

| Common failure | Better practice |
|:--|:--|
| Committees have unclear responsibilities | Define and approve committee charters |
| Important risks are reported too late | Establish clear escalation criteria |
| Board reports contain too much technical detail | Focus on business impact and required decisions |
| Corrective actions have no owner | Assign accountable executives and deadlines |
| Policies exist but are not applied | Verify implementation and effectiveness |
| Board members rarely challenge management | Encourage informed, constructive challenge |
